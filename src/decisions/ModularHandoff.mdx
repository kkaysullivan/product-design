# Modular Handoff

Long intake/referral experiences should not live inside articles. Two
independent triggers move a Modular experience into a focused SPA —
either one is enough on its own.

## Trigger: step count

- 1 step — stays inline. A single question that resolves in place is fine
  to complete right where it's embedded.
- 2+ steps — hand off. If a Modular intake/referral experience has more
  than one step, move the customer into a focused SPA rather than
  completing the full flow inside the article.
- The embedded piece becomes a lightweight launcher into the SPA, not a
  stepper that runs to completion — the same pattern Real Estate already
  uses.

## Trigger: assessments

- Assessments always hand off, independent of step count — even a
  single-question assessment moves. The reason is different from the
  step-count trigger: assessments ask for attention and potentially
  thoughtful or stressful answers, and shouldn't have to compete with
  everything else happening on an article or landing page.

<Canvas of={ModularHandoffStories.AssessmentTrigger} />

## Out of scope: tools

- Calculators and other lightweight tools are explicitly excluded from
  both triggers above. They can still make sense as embedded, Modular
  experiences — regardless of step count — because a customer's intent
  and interaction model with a tool is different from an
  intake/referral/assessment flow.
- Tools can continue to behave differently. This isn't an oversight — it's
  a deliberate carve-out, not a gap to close later.

<Canvas of={ModularHandoffStories.ToolsException} />

## Modular → SPA (hybrid)

- The launcher is the handoff point: one lightweight step (or a single
  "Get Started" action) inside the article, then straight into a SPA for
  the remaining steps. See
  [Foundations/Experience Types](?path=/docs/foundations-experience-types--docs).

<Canvas of={ModularHandoffStories.LauncherPattern} />

## History

The team first discussed a 5-question threshold, then moved to a simpler,
stronger rule: any intake/referral experience with more than one step
hands off, regardless of exactly how many questions it has.
