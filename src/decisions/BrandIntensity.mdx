# Brand Intensity

Two independent questions decide how loud the brand gets on any screen —
not one.

- **Bandwidth** — does the user have spare attention right now? Low means
  mid-task, focused, repetitive. High means the task is paused or just
  finished.
- **Stakes** — is this moment emotionally loaded? Low means routine, low
  emotion. High means a big win or bad news — either way, it matters.

## High stakes + low bandwidth — highest-risk quadrant

Recede visually. Dial warmth up in copy.

e.g. reviewing a denial, a number that scares the user.

Don't soften bad news with decoration — soften it with direct, empathetic
language. Ambient texture is still OK (see the test below).

<Canvas of={BrandIntensityStories.HighestRisk} />

## High stakes + high bandwidth — signature-moment territory

Celebrate the win, out loud.

e.g. debt paid off, the debt-free scream, a milestone hit.

This is where 1-2 signature interactions should live, not scattered
everywhere.

<Canvas of={BrandIntensityStories.SignatureMoment} />

## Low stakes + low bandwidth

Recede + plain tone.

e.g. updating an address.

Design-system tokens only — no illustration, no custom motion. Ambient
texture is still OK (see the test below).

## Low stakes + high bandwidth

Light presence, optional.

e.g. browsing options. Some personality is welcome.

## The ambient texture test

"Recede" ≠ zero brand. A quiet background wash or brush-stroke texture can
stay in any quadrant — even the low-bandwidth ones — if it passes every
check below:

1. **Is it passive** — no motion, no animation.
2. **Is it background only** — sits behind content, never becomes the focal
   point.
3. **Is it from the design system** — an approved token/pattern, not a
   one-off asset.

Fails any one of these → it's not ambient anymore, it's active decoration.
That only belongs in the signature-moment box.

## Sources

Each source grounds its own piece of the model, not the pairing as a whole.

- **Bandwidth axis** — NN/g, "Minimize Cognitive Load to Maximize
  Usability": decorative elements measurably slow users down when
  processing capacity is already spent on the task.
- **High-bandwidth / resolved-stakes cell (signature moments)** — the
  Peak-End Rule (Kahneman, via Laws of UX): people judge an experience
  mainly by its peak and its ending, which is why milestone/completion
  moments are the highest-leverage spot for a branded payoff.
- **High-stakes cell (recede + dial up warmth)** — constructive,
  "Designing for Stress," and trauma-informed content design literature
  (UX Content Collective): both argue for clean layouts and plain,
  empathetic language under stress.
